# Publications

1. Grońska-Pęski, M., Acosta-Rodríguez, V. A., **Srinivasa, A.**, Garrido, A., Picciotto, S., Green, C. B., Takahashi, J. S., Shoag, J. E., & Evrony, G. D. (2026). **Caloric restriction modulates genome-wide somatic mutation in mice.** *Cell*.  
   This study used high-fidelity duplex DNA sequencing to demonstrate that caloric restriction reduces somatic mutation burdens across multiple tissues and cell types in mice, establishing genomic integrity as a modifiable component of aging.  
   [Read the full paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.013).

2. Grońska-Pęski, M., **Srinivasa, A.**, & Evrony, G. D. (2026). **Divergent somatic mutation patterns among human cerebellar neuron types.** *Neuron*.  
   This study characterized age-related somatic mutations in human cerebellar Purkinje and granule neurons using high-fidelity duplex sequencing. It revealed important similarities and differences in mutation rates and patterns across neuronal types, advancing understanding of genomic integrity in brain aging and disease.  
   [Read the full paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.022).

3. Shoag, J. E., **Srinivasa, A.†**, Loh, C. A.†, Liu, M. H.†, Lassen, E.†, Melanaphy, S., Costa, B. M., Grońska-Pęski, M., Jabara, N. T., Picciotto, S., Choi, U., Bohorquez, A. D., Barbieri, C. E., Callum, P., Skytte, A. B., & Evrony, G. D. (2025). **Direct measurement of the male germline mutation rate in individuals using sequential sperm samples.** *Nature Communications, 16*, 2546.  
   This study used high-fidelity duplex sequencing of sequential sperm samples collected 10–33 years apart to directly measure individual male germline mutation rates. The findings showed that sperm mutations increase with age while early clonal mutations remain remarkably stable over time.  
   [Read the full paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57507-0).

   † Equal contribution.

4. Narsing Rao, M. P., Dong, Z. Y., **Amoolya, S.**, Neelavar, S., Liu, B. B., Guo, S. X., Hozzein, W. N., & Li, W. J. (2020). **Description of *Paenibacillus yunnanensis* sp. nov., isolated from a tepid spring.** *Current Microbiology, 77*(10), 3174–3178.  
   This study used phenotypic, chemotaxonomic, phylogenetic, and genomic analyses to characterize a bacterium isolated from tepid-spring sediment in Yunnan, China, and establish it as the novel species *Paenibacillus yunnanensis*.  
   [Read the full paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00284-020-02087-z).

## Consortium Publications

1. The Somatic Mosaicism across Human Tissues Network. (2026). **Integrated map of somatic mosaicism across human tissues in 25 individuals.** *bioRxiv*.  
   As a contributing member of the NIH SMaHT Network, I supported this consortium study integrating short- and long-read sequencing, duplex sequencing, single-cell genomics, transcriptomics, and epigenomics across tissue samples. The study establishes a comprehensive baseline of somatic mosaicism across the human body and its relationships with development, aging, and disease.  
   [Read the full preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.01.748636).
