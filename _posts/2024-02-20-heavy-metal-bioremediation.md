Industrial activity, mining, and wastewater discharge have led to widespread **heavy metal contamination in soil and aquatic ecosystems**. Metals such as cadmium, lead, nickel, and copper accumulate in biological systems and pose serious environmental and health risks.

Traditional remediation techniques are often expensive and inefficient, motivating the development of **biological and genetic engineering approaches** for sustainable environmental remediation.

This project investigated two biological strategies for heavy metal remediation:

1. **Engineered microbial systems**
2. **Genetically modified plants for phytoremediation**

---

## Microbial Bioremediation Using Engineered Yeast

One strategy explored the use of genetically engineered yeast to remove heavy metals from wastewater through **biomineralization processes**.

The approach involved modifying the **sulfate assimilation pathway** to increase hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) production.

Hydrogen sulfide reacts with dissolved metal ions to form **insoluble metal sulfides**, effectively precipitating metals from solution.

### Figure – Engineered Sulfate Assimilation Pathway

![Engineering yeast sulfate assimilation pathway](/assets/images/projects/bioremediation/yeast_pathway.png)

Experimental observations demonstrated:

- Efficient precipitation of cadmium and other metals
- Formation of nanoscale metal sulfide particles
- Improved metal recovery efficiency through metabolic engineering

## Phytoremediation Using Genetically Modified Plants

Another approach focused on enhancing heavy metal accumulation in plants using **genetic engineering techniques**.

Plants expressing **ATP sulfurylase**, a key enzyme involved in sulfur assimilation pathways, showed improved metal tolerance and accumulation capacity.

### Figure – Metal Uptake in Transgenic Plants

![Heavy metal accumulation in transgenic plants](/assets/images/projects/bioremediation/metal_accumulation.png)

Experimental results indicated:

- Increased tolerance to multiple metals including Cd, Pb, Ni, Cu, and V
- Enhanced accumulation of metals in plant tissues
- Increased translocation of metals from roots to shoots

## Significance

These results highlight the potential of **synthetic biology and genetic engineering approaches** to improve environmental remediation technologies.

Engineered microorganisms and plants offer promising solutions for:

- Sustainable wastewater treatment
- Recovery of valuable metals
- Reduction of environmental pollution
- Development of scalable bioremediation strategies

By leveraging biological systems for metal detoxification and accumulation, these approaches represent an environmentally friendly alternative to conventional remediation methods.
